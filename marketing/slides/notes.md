## Slide 1 — Title

We're the team behind Regulars. I'm Uzair, and with me are Moiz, Zain and Ali. We built Regulars, a member-retention dashboard for independent gyms in the US, around one goal: turn new members into regulars. In the next 3 minutes: why the first 90 days matter, what the app does, and the price we'd test.

## Slide 2 — The problem

Here's the problem. 6 in 10 new members quit within three months. That's from Sperandei and colleagues, 2016. And they rarely quit on day one. They fade, one missed week at a time. You can see it in this grid. Each square is a day, and this member's visits thin out, then stop.

## Slide 3 — The line

Here is who stays. Members who stay hit 3 visits a week. In a 2001 UK industry study, members who came 3 or more times a week in their first month renewed 78% of the time. Under once a week, it was 59%. We built the app on that gap. 3 visits a week is the line: 3 or more last week is on the line, 1 or 2 is below it, and two weeks in a row under 2 visits is slipping.

## Slide 4 — Meet Regulars

Meet Regulars: one board for every new member's first 90 days. This is the real app, with sample data. Each row is a new member, and each square is a week. Dark is 3 or more visits, grey is one or two, and a red edge is slipping. With Regulars, you see every new member's first 90 days, spot anyone who is slipping, and nudge them back with a personal text.

## Slide 5 — How it works

It works in three steps. Press once for each; the screens come in one at a time.

Track. A member checks in at the front-desk kiosk with their phone number, and the welcome shows their week. Every new member's check-ins land on one board, for 90 days. (Press.)

Flag. Two weeks in a row under 2 visits and they're flagged as slipping, right on Today, while there is still time. (Press.)

Nudge. One tap opens a personal text from you or their coach, ready to send. The app never sends anything itself: the coach sends it from their own phone.

## Slide 6 — Price

Here's the plan we'd test: a free 90-day pilot, then $99 a month per gym. It's not on sale yet. One flat price, with unlimited members. Most US members pay under $50 a month, according to the Health and Fitness Association. So keep 2 members a month, and from the second month it pays for itself.

## Slide 7 — The market

Now the market. There are 55,294 gyms and studios in the US, from the 2024 U.S. Health and Fitness Consumer Report. At $99 a month, that's $1,188 a year per gym. One percent of US gyms is about 553 gyms, or about $657,000 a year. And here's where we fit. Some gym systems do flag members at risk, often on their bigger plans. Ours is the narrow version: only a new member's first 90 days, one rule anyone can check, and messages from the coach's own phone.
